Privacy & safe use

What we collect and why

Your username, email and a password hash support your account. Item listings describe belongings received by staff. Private identifying details, claim evidence and review messages support staff assessment. Collection authorisations confirm a particular handover.

Who can see what

Public visitors see only item listings. Claimants see their own evidence and outcomes. Property officers and administrators can read claims and private item details after completing authenticator verification. Administrators also see application security events and manage account access.

Protection and its limits

Private identifying details, claim evidence, review messages and authenticator setup secrets are encrypted in the database. Passwords are hashed; collection and recovery codes are stored as digests. A server with access to the encryption key can decrypt private records. Account security pages require HTTPS. Use the local HTTPS profile for staff access; its certificate needs explicit client trust.

Account recovery

Save recovery codes privately outside this browser. A code can finish a second-factor check or reset your account password and authenticator. Each code works once. Full recovery invalidates all the other recovery codes. Your email address alone cannot reset your account. If you lose both your authenticator and saved codes, contact the local operator for an identity review.

Please keep sensitive information out

  • Use fictional data while testing.
  • Never submit passwords, device PINs, identity numbers, payment details, or other people's personal information as claim evidence.
  • Describe identifying marks privately; don't put them in public item descriptions.
  • Give collection codes only to an authorised property officer during collection. Replacing a code invalidates the previous code.
  • Sign out on shared devices. No legitimate staff member needs your password, authenticator setup key or recovery codes.

Activity records

We record account security actions, claim outcomes, access denials, staff evidence access and handovers. Audit events exclude claim text, passwords, authenticator secrets and codes, recovery codes and collection codes. Abuse counters use keyed identifiers and short-lived counters rather than storing raw IP addresses or attempted usernames in the application audit trail. The development web server also writes request paths to its console.

Retention and requests

The local operator controls this prototype's data. Contact that operator to request an account/data review. Expired sessions, abuse counters and pending authenticator setups can be pruned. There is no self-service deletion or general personal-data retention schedule in this version.
